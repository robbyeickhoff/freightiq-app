# Downtown Telluride

## Purpose

Document confirmed operational routing knowledge for the Downtown Telluride zone.

This document preserves the repeatable routing patterns used by experienced drivers. It explains how to classify stops, how the zone normally flows, where durable exceptions exist, and how the Downtown Telluride zone connects to the surrounding macro route.

Do not treat today's stop order as permanent unless the operational reason is documented here.

## Zone Characteristics

Downtown Telluride is fundamentally different from Mountain Village.

Mountain Village is organized around an irregular mountain road network.

Downtown Telluride is organized around a connected street grid.

Because most streets connect to one another, routing depends less on dead ends and turnaround locations and more on preserving an efficient progression through the street grid.

The primary routing objectives are:

- maintain forward progression
- minimize unnecessary crossings
- reduce repeated travel
- preserve efficient truck flow

Unlike Mountain Village, dead ends and difficult turnarounds are relatively uncommon.

---

## Operational Philosophy

Downtown Telluride should normally be worked in one continuous visit.

The preferred progression is:

```text
Zone 1 — South
→ Zone 2 — East
→ Zone 3 — Central / North
```

This sequence preserves efficient movement through town while minimizing repeated travel across the grid.

Appointments, freight accessibility, customer requirements, trailer loading, weather, or documented operational exceptions may justify local changes.

Such changes should remain local whenever practical.

Avoid reversing the overall Downtown Telluride flow unless there is a significant operational benefit.

```text
Lawson Hill / Society

```

Complete Downtown Telluride before entering Airport / Aldasoro whenever practical.

## Entry

Operational entry begins as Highway 145 transitions into Colorado Avenue.

This marks the transition from Mountain Village into Downtown Telluride.

Highway 145 and W Highway 145 Spur refer to the same inbound corridor.

Although a small number of deliveries may occur on the south side of this corridor, it primarily functions as the operational entrance into Downtown Telluride.

---

## Exit

Operational exit occurs when Downtown Telluride deliveries are complete and the truck continues east toward the Airport / Aldasoro zone.

---

## Internal Flow

Downtown Telluride consists of three operational Micro Zones.

```text
Zone 1 — South
→ Zone 2 — East
→ Zone 3 — Central / North
```

Each Micro Zone represents a logical portion of the Downtown street grid.

The preferred order minimizes repeated crossings and preserves continuous truck flow through town.

---

### Operational Characteristics

Zone 1 establishes the south-side progression through Downtown Telluride.

Unlike Mountain Village, these streets are generally connected through the town grid and rarely require special turnaround planning.

The objective is to preserve forward movement while avoiding unnecessary returns into completed portions of the south side.

### Preferred Flow

Complete the southern streets before transitioning into Downtown Zone 2.

The exact order depends on:

- active deliveries
- appointments

Highway 145 and W Highway 145 Spur describe the same inbound corridor.

Although a small number of deliveries may occur on the south side of this roadway, it primarily serves as the operational entrance into Downtown Telluride.

Do not allow one or two highway-side stops to disrupt the overall Downtown flow.

### Transition

After completing Zone 1 South, continue into Downtown Zone 2 East.

Avoid returning to completed south-side streets unless required by a significant operational constraint.

## Micro Zone — Zone 2 East

### Purpose

Zone 2 East contains the eastern portion of the Downtown Telluride street grid.

This Micro Zone is defined partly by street names and partly by block ranges.

The primary objective is to work the eastern grid efficiently before returning to the central and northern portions of town.

### Included Roads and Areas

1. E Colorado Ave — 300 block and east
2. Pandora Ln
3. Liberty Bell Ln
4. Primrose Ln
5. E Columbia Ave — 300 block and east
6. Wilkins Way
7. Shadow Ln
8. Laurel St
9. N Pinon St
10. E Pandora Ave
11. Hemlock St
12. N Maple St
13. N Alder St

### Operational Characteristics

Zone 2 East is a connected street-grid area.

Most streets connect to another road, so route quality depends more on preserving grid progression than on managing dead ends or turnaround limits.

The 300-block thresholds on E Colorado Avenue and E Columbia Avenue define the western edge of this Micro Zone.

### Preferred Flow

Enter Zone 2 East after completing Zone 1 South.

Work the active eastern streets while minimizing repeated travel across the same blocks.

The exact order depends on:

- active deliveries
- customer availability
- appointments
- freight accessibility
- temporary access conditions

Preserve continuous movement through the eastern grid whenever practical.

### Block-Boundary Rules

- E Colorado Ave addresses in the 300 block and east belong to Zone 2 East.
- E Columbia Ave addresses in the 300 block and east belong to Zone 2 East.
- Lower block ranges belong to Zone 3 Central / North.

Use the physical address block, not only the consignee name or mailing label, when classifying the stop.

### Transition

After completing Zone 2 East, continue into Zone 3 Central / North.

Avoid returning to completed eastern streets unless a significant operational constraint requires it.

### Purpose

Zone 3 Central / North contains the core commercial grid, the central and northern streets, and the hillside roads at the north and west edges of Downtown Telluride.

This Micro Zone is normally completed after Zone 2 East and before transitioning to Airport / Aldasoro.

### Operational Characteristics

Zone 3 combines two related operating areas:

- the central and north-side Downtown street grid
- the hillside roads extending beyond the main grid

The central grid is generally well connected.

The hillside roads may require more deliberate sequencing because they sit farther from the core streets and can create unnecessary returns if serviced too early.

### Block-Boundary Rules

- E Colorado Ave addresses in the 100 and 200 blocks belong to Zone 3.
- E Columbia Ave addresses in the 100 and 200 blocks belong to Zone 3.
- W Colorado Ave addresses in the 100 through 500 blocks belong to Zone 3.
- E Colorado Ave and E Columbia Ave addresses in the 300 block and east belong to Zone 2 East.

Use the physical block location when classifying a stop.

### Preferred Flow

Enter Zone 3 after completing Zone 2 East.

Work the central and north-side grid before finishing with any active hillside roads when that preserves the cleanest exit.

The exact order depends on:

- active deliveries
- customer availability
- appointments
- freight accessibility
- street access
- the location of the next active Airport / Aldasoro stop

Avoid unnecessary movement between the hillside roads and the central grid.

### Hillside Transition

The hillside roads include:

- Cornet St
- Butcher Creek Dr
- Hillside Ln
- N Sunset Ridge Dr
- S Sunset Ridge Dr
- Pilot Knob Ln

Treat these as the outer portion of Zone 3.

When active, sequence them so the final Downtown stop leaves the truck positioned for the most efficient transition toward Airport / Aldasoro.

### Transition

After completing Zone 3 Central / North, continue to Airport / Aldasoro.

Avoid re-entering completed Downtown streets unless a significant operational constraint requires it.

## Right-Turn and Directional Preferences

Downtown Telluride routing should preserve one continuous progression through the street grid whenever practical.

Preferred pattern:

```text
Zone 1 South
→ Zone 2 East
→ Zone 3 Central / North
→ Airport / Aldasoro
```

The objective is to reduce:

- repeated crossings
- unnecessary returns to completed blocks
- avoidable backtracking
- inefficient movement between the central grid and hillside roads

The preferred pattern may be adjusted for appointments, freight accessibility, temporary street access, safety, or customer requirements.

---

## Transition Traps

### Re-entering completed streets

Leaving one Downtown Micro Zone and returning later usually creates unnecessary travel through the grid.

Preferred correction:

```text
Complete the active Micro Zone
→ continue into the next Micro Zone
```

### Crossing repeatedly between east and central blocks

Repeated movement between Zone 2 East and Zone 3 Central / North creates avoidable backtracking.

Use the block boundaries to classify stops correctly before sequencing them.

### Servicing hillside roads too early

The hillside roads sit outside the main central grid.

Servicing them before nearby central and north-side work is complete may force a return into Downtown.

Preferred correction:

```text
Complete the central and north-side grid
→ finish active hillside roads
→ transition toward Airport / Aldasoro
```

## Common Exceptions

The normal Downtown Telluride sequence may change because of:

- appointments
- customer closing times
- freight accessibility
- trailer loading
- customer availability
- temporary road closures
- construction
- event traffic
- weather
- truck-access restrictions

Most exceptions should remain local.

Avoid reversing the full Downtown flow unless the operational benefit clearly outweighs the disruption.

---

## Road Conditions and Access

Downtown Telluride is generally more connected and less access-constrained than Mountain Village.

Most streets connect to another road, so dead ends and turnaround traps are relatively uncommon.

Document exceptions individually when they involve:

- one-way restrictions
- seasonal closures
- construction
- narrow alleys
- loading-zone limits
- temporary event restrictions
- unusual truck fit

Stop-specific access belongs in customer intel unless it establishes a durable zone-level rule.
