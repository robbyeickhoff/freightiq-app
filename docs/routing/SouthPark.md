# South Park

## Purpose

This document defines the operational routing knowledge for the South Park zone on the inbound Telluride corridor.

South Park is primarily a transition zone. The main objective is to complete its active stops while traveling into Telluride and avoid returning later.

---

## Status

Draft based on repeated real-world route reviews with Robby Eickhoff.

---

## Zone Classification

The South Park zone includes:

- Two Rivers Dr.
- County Road 63L
- South Park Rd.
- Vance Dr.
- Nimbus Dr. / Nimbus neighborhood

These locations may use a Telluride mailing address but should not be classified as Downtown Telluride.

---

## Macro Position

Default flow:

```text
Placerville / Sawpit
→ South Park
→ Lawson Hill / Society
→ Mountain Village
→ Downtown Telluride
```

The most important rule is:

> Complete active South Park stops on the way into Telluride whenever practical.

Avoid leaving the zone unfinished and returning later.

---

## Internal Flow

Preferred core order:

```text
County Road 63L
→ South Park Rd.
→ Vance Dr.
```

When active, Two Rivers generally precedes the core sequence and Nimbus generally follows it:

```text
Two Rivers
→ County Road 63L
→ South Park Rd.
→ Vance Dr.
→ Nimbus
```

This sequence favors right-hand turns and smoother truck movement.

It is a dependable preference, but not a rigid rule. Traffic is generally light, so trailer loading or customer needs may justify small changes.

---

## Residential Areas

Two Rivers and Nimbus are residential areas with relatively infrequent deliveries.

Their presence should not control the entire zone sequence.

Stop-specific details such as:

- Truck fit
- Turnaround space
- Customer meeting points
- Highway pullout procedures
- Access restrictions

belong in customer intel, not as zone-wide rules.

---

## Road Conditions

Much of County Road 63L is dirt.

Any seasonal, weather, or customer-specific access concerns should be documented at the stop level unless they apply to the entire road.

---

## Core Rule

South Park does not need to be over-optimized.

A successful route:

- Classifies South Park correctly.
- Completes it on the inbound Telluride corridor.
- Avoids unnecessary zone re-entry.
- Uses the preferred internal order when practical.
