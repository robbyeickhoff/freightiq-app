# Log Hill

## Purpose

This document defines confirmed operational routing knowledge for the Log Hill zone.

Log Hill is operationally separate from Ridgway Proper even though many addresses use Ridgway as the mailing city. Classify stops by the physical road network and actual route flow, not by postal city alone.

## Zone Classification

Treat a stop as part of Log Hill when its physical road belongs to the Log Hill operational road network, even when the manifest lists Ridgway as the city.

The operational Log Hill zone is broader than the formal Loghill Village boundary shown on maps. The formal boundary is a useful visual reference, but it does not define the complete driver-use zone.

Major references:

- CR 1 is the primary north-south spine.
- CR 22A is the approximate northern operational boundary.
- Ponderosa Drive surrounds the main residential golf-course neighborhood and contains the majority of Log Hill deliveries.
- Highway 62 is the normal southwest transition toward Placerville, Telluride, and the remaining westbound route.

Neighboring operational zones include:

- Ridgway Proper
- Ridgway-address stops north of Highway 62
- Placerville / Sawpit

## Authoritative Boundary Definition

The written road and road-segment list is authoritative for zone classification.

Maps are supporting visual references. If a map and the written definition disagree, use the written definition until the discrepancy is reviewed and corrected.

For V1:

- CR 22A is the approximate northern boundary of the greater Log Hill zone.
- The operational CR 1 corridor begins approximately at CR 22A and continues south through the Log Hill road network.
- The southern and western transition is toward Highway 62 through CR 24, CR 24A, or CR 24C.
- The formal Loghill Village boundary does not limit the broader operational zone.

Zone membership is durable. The timing and direction used to service an edge stop may change according to the next active stop and freight accessibility.

## Boundary and Transition Logic

Assign each stop an authoritative home zone based on its physical road position and normal routing behavior.

Do not reclassify a stop solely to fit today's route.

### Northern CR 1 edge transition

Stops near the CR 22A northern edge can create a route-dependent choice:

- Continue south on CR 1 into Log Hill, or
- Double back north toward Highway 550.

Choose between those options according to:

- The next active stop
- The next active zone
- Freight accessibility in the trailer
- Whether continuing south preserves the day's forward flow

The stop remains classified in its authoritative home zone regardless of which direction is used afterward.

### Edge Transition Example

- **Road or area:** CR 1 near CR 22A
- **Home zone:** Log Hill
- **Edge connection:** Highway 550 to the north; main Log Hill road network to the south
- **Best when next active zone is south or west:** Continue south on CR 1
- **Best service direction:** Manifest- and trailer-dependent
- **Backtracking risk:** Continuing south can be wrong when the next accessible freight requires a northbound return; doubling back can be wrong when the remaining work continues through Log Hill
- **Valid alternate:** Return north to Highway 550 when the next stop and freight accessibility justify it


Complete Ridgway Proper before entering Log Hill when traveling west. Finish Log Hill positioned southwest toward Highway 62 and Telluride.

## Entry Points

### Normal westbound entry

Enter Log Hill from the Highway 62 / county-road approach after completing Ridgway Proper.

### Northern entry

CR 1 connects Highway 550 near Colona to the northern edge of the Log Hill operational zone. CR 22A is the approximate northern boundary.

The northern entry is route-dependent and may create a decision to continue south through Log Hill or return north to Highway 550 after an edge stop.

## Exit Points

The normal westbound objective is to leave Log Hill positioned southwest toward Highway 62 and Telluride.

Typical transition:

```text
Ponderosa Drive area
→ CR 1
→ CR 24 / CR 24A / CR 24C
→ Highway 62
```

Drivers may choose CR 24, CR 24A, or CR 24C according to active stops, road conditions, and personal preference. This document does not prescribe one connector because no durable operational reason currently requires it.

When the route's next accessible freight or active stop is northbound, returning toward Highway 550 may be valid.

## Internal Flow

Log Hill usually has only a small number of active deliveries, commonly about two to five. A rigid full-zone sequence is therefore not appropriate.

Confirmed working rules:

- Keep stops on the same branch or Micro Zone together when practical.
- Avoid descending toward Highway 62 only to climb back into another Log Hill branch.
- Sequence the final stop to create the cleanest transition toward the next active zone.
- Do not force a rigid order when two sequences are operationally equivalent.
- Optimize the active stops rather than driving the complete road network.

### Ponderosa Drive working pattern

- Ponderosa Drive surrounds the main residential golf-course neighborhood.
- The majority of Log Hill deliveries occur in this area.
- The southern end is the usual entry point approximately 80 percent of the time.
- The usual movement is generally counterclockwise, but this is a loose preference rather than a required full-loop pattern.
- The driver rarely completes the entire loop unless active stops make that efficient.
- When no active stop requires continuing around the loop, the driver commonly exits through the same southern end.
- The final direction remains stop-dependent and should support the next zone.

## Right-Turn and Crossing Preferences

When both Ridgway Proper and Log Hill stops are active on a westbound Telluride route:

```text
Ridgway Proper
→ Log Hill
→ continue west
```

Complete Ridgway Proper first when practical. Reversing the order can force an unnecessary left turn back onto Highway 62, backtracking into Ridgway, and repeated highway travel.

Within Log Hill, choose the final stop and exit according to where the decision leaves the truck for the next active zone.

## Transition Traps

### Log Hill before unfinished Ridgway Proper

This can create:

- An unnecessary left turn back onto Highway 62
- Backtracking into Ridgway
- Repeated travel over the same corridor

Preferred correction:

```text
Finish Ridgway Proper
→ enter Log Hill
→ exit toward Placerville / Telluride
```

### Descending before another Log Hill branch

Do not descend toward Highway 62 only to climb back into another active Log Hill branch when the stops can be grouped efficiently.

### Northern edge decision made without checking freight

At a CR 1 / CR 22A-area stop, do not automatically continue south or automatically return to Highway 550. The correct transition depends on the next active stop and whether its freight is accessible.

## Road Conditions

- The back half and north side of Ponderosa Drive are dirt.
- Dirt surface alone is not a reason to avoid that portion.
- Use the paved or dirt path that is most efficient for the active stops and the next route transition.
- Do not complete the entire Ponderosa loop merely for geographic completeness.

Additional winter conditions, steep grades, turnaround limits, and large-trailer restrictions remain undocumented.

## Common Exceptions

The normal zone flow may change because of:

- Trailer loading or freight accessibility
- Freight size
- Appointment or closing time
- Pickup timing
- Road closure or weather
- Customer availability
- The location of the next active stop

These constraints should normally change one stop or one local branch rather than disrupt the full macro-zone flow.

## Customer-Level Details That Do Not Belong Here

Store the following in customer intel unless they establish a durable zone-level rule:

- Dock instructions
- Gate codes
- Exact turnaround limits
- Customer meeting points
- Receiving hours
- Stop-specific truck fit

## Core Zone Rule

> Complete Ridgway Proper before Log Hill when traveling west, then work only the active Log Hill branches and finish positioned for the next zone. At the northern CR 1 edge, choose whether to continue south or return to Highway 550 according to the next stop and accessible freight.
