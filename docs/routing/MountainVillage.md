## Zone Characteristics

Mountain Village is a one-way-in, one-way-out delivery zone.

The preferred internal flow is west to east.

Under normal conditions:

- Enter from the west.
- Work south-side stops while traveling east.
- Continue to the easternmost active stop.
- Turn around.
- Work north-side stops while traveling west toward the exit.

In an ideal route with an optimal trailer load, this minimizes:

- zigzagging
- unnecessary backtracking
- repeated crossings
- unnecessary truck maneuvering

This is the preferred operational pattern rather than an absolute rule.

Appointments, freight accessibility, trailer loading, customer requirements, weather, and documented operational exceptions may justify local changes.

---

## Operational Philosophy

Mountain Village is fundamentally different from Downtown Telluride.

Mountain Village consists of an irregular mountain-road network with numerous residential branches, dead ends, steep grades, and limited turnaround locations.

Successful routing depends more on:

- preserving directional flow
- grouping nearby roads
- understanding truck access
- minimizing unnecessary climbing and backtracking

than on minimizing total mileage.

The goal is not simply to visit every road.

The goal is to preserve efficient truck flow through the zone while positioning the truck for the next Macro Zone.

## Entry

Operational entry occurs at the Mountain Village highway turnoff.

The large Mountain Village entrance sign marks the beginning of the operational zone.

This is where routing transitions from highway travel into Mountain Village internal routing.

---

## Exit

Operational exit occurs when returning to the same Mountain Village entrance and rejoining Highway 145.

Passing the entrance sign while leaving the village marks completion of the Mountain Village zone and transition toward Downtown Telluride.

## Internal Flow

Mountain Village is built from seven operational Micro Zones.

The normal progression is:

```text
Ski Ranch South
→ Ski Ranch North
→ Mountain Village West
→ Benchmark
→ San Joaquin
→ Mountain Village East
→ Mountain Village North
```

This sequence represents the preferred operational flow when the zone is fully active.

Individual stops may be resequenced because of:

- appointments
- freight accessibility
- trailer loading
- safety
- customer availability
- documented operational exceptions

Local changes should remain local whenever practical.

Do not reverse the overall Mountain Village flow unless the operational benefit clearly outweighs the disruption.

| Road | Characteristic |
|------|----------------|
| Arizona St | Dead end — tight turnaround |
| Touchdown Dr | Turn around at the top where Highlands Way begins |
| Upper Touchdown Dr | Dead end — back down |
| Highlands Way | Dead end — back down |

### Operational Exceptions

#### Victoria Drive

Victoria Drive is located on the north side of Mountain Village Boulevard.

Under the normal Mountain Village flow, north-side roads would typically be worked on the westbound return.

Victoria Drive is the documented exception.

A low skier bridge prevents a practical westbound approach.

Service Victoria Drive while traveling eastbound by backing in directly from Mountain Village Boulevard.

The preferred sequence is driven by truck access and backing safety rather than side-of-road flow.

### Preferred Flow

Maintain eastbound progression through Mountain Village Boulevard.

Apply the Victoria Drive exception without disrupting the remainder of the Mountain Village flow.

Complete the Micro Zone positioned for Benchmark.

## Physical Location Over Consignee Name

Route the physical delivery location, not only the customer, contractor, or consignee name shown on the manifest.

A contractor or interior designer may be receiving freight at a larger property, hotel, residence, or jobsite.

### Confirmed Example

- **457 Mountain Village Boulevard** is **Mountain Lodge**, a large hotel.
- A manifest may show another business or contractor name, such as an interior designer working inside one of the units.
- For routing purposes, classify and reason about the stop as **Mountain Lodge**.

Do not create permanent routing rules around a temporary consignee name.

## Confirmed Operational Exception: Mountain Lodge

### Default Geographic Position

Mountain Lodge is on the north side of Mountain Village Boulevard.

Under the default Mountain Village flow, a north-side stop would normally be completed westbound after reaching the easternmost active stop and turning around.

### Backing Constraint

The Mountain Lodge Delivery Zone requires a tricky back-in.

The back-in is safer and easier when the truck approaches while facing east because this avoids a blind-side backing maneuver.

### Routing Rule

Mountain Lodge may be serviced eastbound before the normal north-side return sequence when doing so provides the safer sight-side backing approach.

This is a documented operational exception to the default side-of-road flow.

The reason for the exception is:

- safer backing
- better visibility
- easier truck positioning

The rule is **not** that Mountain Lodge is always second, always early, or always serviced before every other stop.

Its placement still depends on:

- today's active stops
- current direction of travel
- appointments
- access conditions
- other confirmed operational constraints

## Transition Traps

### Zigzagging Across the Village

The most common routing mistake is repeatedly crossing between the south and north sides of Mountain Village Boulevard.

Although this may not ruin the day's route, it creates unnecessary:

- mileage
- truck maneuvering
- repeated travel
- loss of operational efficiency

Preserve the overall directional flow whenever practical.

### Breaking Zone Integrity

Mountain Village should normally be completed during one continuous visit.

Leaving the zone and returning later usually creates unnecessary travel and disrupts the preferred macro flow.

Exceptions may include:

- appointments
- freight accessibility
- customer availability
- road closures
- severe weather

### Ignoring Documented Operational Exceptions

The preferred directional flow is not absolute.

Examples include:

- Victoria Drive
- Cortina Drive
- Mountain Lodge
- future documented truck-access exceptions

Truck access, backing safety, and maneuverability take priority over ideal geographic sequencing.

## Winter Operations

Mountain Village should be treated as a chain-required zone whenever winter traction conditions warrant.

Field experience indicates chains are required on most winter delivery days.

The most common winter mistake is delaying chain installation in an attempt to save time.

Doing so frequently results in:

- loss of traction
- becoming stuck
- blocking traffic
- significantly larger delays than the time required to install chains before entering the zone

Default principle:

Install chains before entering Mountain Village whenever traction conditions indicate they are needed.

Driver judgment always applies.
