# Driver Feedback & Validation

## Purpose

FreightIQ should be shaped by real drivers solving real delivery problems.

Assumptions are valuable for generating ideas, but real-world validation determines what becomes part of the product.

## Core Principles

### Drivers Are the Source of Truth

The best product decisions come from observing real drivers using FreightIQ during real deliveries.

### Validate Before Expanding

Before building major new features, confirm that existing workflows solve the intended problem well.

### Feedback Over Opinions

Individual opinions are valuable, but recurring patterns observed across multiple drivers should carry the most weight.

### Real Work Beats Artificial Testing

The most valuable feedback comes from FreightIQ being used during actual deliveries, where time pressure, distractions, and imperfect conditions naturally expose friction.

## Using These Principles

Driver feedback should inform every major product decision, but no single conversation should dictate the direction of FreightIQ.

Look for recurring patterns, validate them in real-world use, and make deliberate improvements based on evidence rather than assumptions.

Meaningful validation is an ongoing process, not a milestone to complete.

## Decision Heuristics

When evaluating driver feedback or considering a new feature, ask these questions:

- Is this solving a real problem or a hypothetical one?
- Have multiple drivers experienced this issue?
- Have we observed this behavior in real-world use?
- Would improving an existing workflow create more value than adding a new feature?
- Does this feedback align with FreightIQ's long-term vision?
- Can this change be validated before expanding further?

If the answer to several of these questions is "no," continue observing before committing development effort.

## FreightIQ Examples

These principles have repeatedly shaped the way FreightIQ has evolved.

### Route Building Practice

Many routing principles were not invented in planning sessions—they were discovered through years of delivering freight and refined through repeated real-world observation.

### Delivery Zone

Renaming Truck Entrance to Delivery Zone reflected the language drivers naturally understand, reducing confusion without adding functionality.

### Help Center

The Help Center grew from recognizing that onboarding should introduce FreightIQ, while deeper learning should be available when drivers actually need it.

### Product Direction

Several planned features have been intentionally delayed because real-world validation showed that improving existing workflows would provide greater value than expanding the feature set.

## The Standard

Driver feedback is one of FreightIQ's greatest competitive advantages, but it must be paired with thoughtful product judgment.

Listen carefully. Observe objectively. Validate consistently.

The goal is not to build every feature drivers request.

The goal is to build the product drivers would create if they had years to compare experiences, recognize patterns, and work together to solve the problems they all share.
